What the data shows. On three of the four tasks (the data cleaning, the Python bug fix and the TypeScript bug fix) all seven setups passed the hidden check every time: 35 of 35 runs (the data task once each, the two bug fixes twice each). Those tasks do not separate anyone on correctness. Every miss in the whole pilot is on the fourth task, the eleven-file lint chore, and every one of them is a run that was killed at its token cap, not a run that finished and failed. Five of those eight cap hits (Sol and Astra twice each, Opus once) had already left work that passed the hidden check; the two Opus + Haiku swarm runs hit their 400,000-token cap and one Haiku run its 150,000 cap with the check still failing; Haiku and Opus were each capped once and passed once on that task, Opus at 166,361 tokens against a 148,929-token pass. So the pass column for that task mostly measures who finishes inside 150,000 tokens, and Codex counts cached input in that total, which makes the cap bite it earlier. A different cap would reorder the table.

Solo against team against swarm. With n = 2 per cell this is a hint, not a result. The Opus + Sonnet team passed 7 of 7 against 6 of 7 for Opus alone, but the whole gap is one Opus run that was 16,000 tokens over the cap, and the team cost about 1.4 times as much per solved run ($0.41 against $0.29) and was slower on three of the four tasks (70 s against 55 s on average). Sonnet alone also passed 7 of 7, at $0.07 per solved run, so on this sample the team added cost and time over a cheaper solo model without a visible gain. The Opus + Haiku swarm was the most expensive per solved run ($0.57), the slowest, and its two capped runs on the lint chore left work that failed the hidden check (as did one Haiku run), unlike the Codex and Opus cap hits; on the three easy tasks its ten workers produced the same passes as Haiku alone at roughly 60 to 120 times the cost. Nothing here shows that parallel workers help on a task that is genuinely large; the one task with breadth (eleven files) was small enough for Sonnet alone to finish in about 90,000 tokens.

The five single models. All five solved the three easy tasks every time. Cost per solved run ran from $0.007 (Haiku 5.5) through $0.07 (Sonnet 5.5), $0.08 (Sol 6.1) and $0.29 (Opus 5.5) to $0.53 (Astra 6), so the pass counts do not justify the order-of-magnitude price gaps on tasks this small. Wall times sat between 13 and 81 seconds for solo runs and are too noisy at this size to rank. Diff size is the one visible style difference: Sol and Astra made 2 to 3 changed lines on the two bug-fix tasks against the upstream fixes' 2, while Opus and Sonnet wrote 21 to 61 lines for the humanize bug the maintainers fixed in two. Both passed the hidden tests, so larger is not wrong, only different.

What it does not show. It does not rank the models, because the discriminating task is decided by a cap rather than by capability; it does not show reliability, because every cell is one or two runs; it says nothing about harder or longer tasks, other languages, other effort levels, or the same models in a different CLI; and the dollar figures are list-price equivalents of subscription runs, not what anyone would be billed. The next step is a harder task set and a looser token cap, chosen before the runs.
